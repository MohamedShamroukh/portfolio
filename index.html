About me

I am a doctoral researcher at Loughborough University working at the intersection of GeoAI, urban analytics, and spatial data science. My research develops data-driven approaches to understand pedestrian activity and support more walkable, inclusive, and sustainable cities. I combine geospatial data, machine learning, computer vision, and sensor-derived datasets to generate actionable evidence for urban planning and decision-making.

Selected projects

Open-source tools and research projects connecting geospatial analysis, machine learning and urban applications.

Urban Similarity Embeddings | Geospatial Foundation Model

Current project developing a geospatial foundation model that learns 64-dimensional similarity embeddings representing the unique character of urban places. These embeddings capture complex urban characteristics in a high-dimensional space and can support a wide range of downstream urban analysis and modelling tasks..

Research & skills

Research interests and technical capabilities across geospatial intelligence, urban analytics and data-driven planning.

Research focus

Core themes connecting GeoAI, human mobility and evidence-based urban planning.

GeoAI and spatial modelling

Developing predictive geospatial models that connect urban form, activity patterns and spatial relationships.

Pedestrian mobility and urban analytics

Understanding where, when and how people move through urban environments using sensor, spatial and contextual data.

Digital trace data

Investigating the reliability, calibration and spatial representativeness of WiFi and other digital mobility data.

Pedestrian-friendly planning

Using evidence from movement and urban form to support more accessible and pedestrian-oriented places.

Remote sensing and computer vision

Applying satellite imagery and object detection to urban and environmental monitoring.

Open-source geospatial tools

Building practical Python and QGIS tools that make geospatial analysis more accessible and reproducible.
